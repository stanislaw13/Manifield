How to use them:
- These were **selected for you**, not broadcast. The research director reads everything the branches submit and decides who needs what, so a finding here was judged relevant to how *your* route should proceed. Other branches have found other things; not seeing them is deliberate, and not evidence that nothing else is happening.
- Treat a finding as evidence to verify, not as authority. A sibling working under a different prior may have been right about the fact and wrong about what it means for your approach.
- A finding marked CONFIRMED has been independently checked — by the mathematician running this project, or by a fresh-context adjudication on an unattended run. You may rely on it without re-deriving it. Anything not marked is a branch's own claim.
- **A routed finding is not permission to abandon your approach.** Your branch exists to work its own route to the point where the evidence settles it. If something here genuinely kills your route, that is a real result: say so plainly in your task summary and let the kill criterion do its job. If it merely makes another approach look more attractive, that is not yours to act on — a sibling is already working it.
- A finding can change what your remaining tasks are worth. Say so in your task summary if it does, rather than continuing as if nothing happened.
- Do not re-derive what is already listed. Cite it and move on.
